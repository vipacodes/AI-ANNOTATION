The part they don't put in the ad

Flags, honeypots, and keeping evidence

On these platforms the thing that ends your income is rarely a bad week. It's a silent account status change with the current period's balance held, no email with specifics, and support that replies from a template queue. This tab explains how the signals are collected, and gives you the one mitigation that has a track record: your own contemporaneous log.

Signal class 1

Behavioural

Clipboard paste into a response field · sub-threshold completion time · idle-but-timer-running · devtools open during a session · keyboard-autocorrect patterns read as auto-typer · tab-away counts. Most false positives here come from a dictionary extension or a slow network, and none of them are explainable in the UI.

Signal class 2

Content

Stylometry drift inside one contributor's history (your writing suddenly gets more even), boilerplate-phrase density, near-duplicate answers across reviewers, and agreement collapse against your co-reviewers on the same item. This is the class that catches people who genuinely write formally — which is why the detector on this site shows its features instead of a black box score.

Signal class 3

Environmental

IP country vs. ID country vs. payout-account country. All three must match. Shared household IPs and VPNs are the two most common reasons an honest contributor is banned; a purchased "US-location account" is a guaranteed ban, because the seller's ID never matches yours.

Why "no explanation" is the design, not the bug

If a platform told you which signal fired, the signal would be trivially defeated. Every marketplace that has answered this in a forum has said a version of "we cannot disclose the markers used to protect platform integrity." Two consequences you can actually plan for:

1
Withdraw or cash out on a short cycle. A balance held in escrow at the moment of a restriction is a balance you negotiate with a bot queue. A balance already in your bank is not.
2
Log as you go, not after. A log written the week a ban happens is treated differently by a payment processor, an employer, or a court than one written three months later.
