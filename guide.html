How the real platforms work

Notes on the actual market, compiled from public rate bands, contributor reports and review sites as of mid-2026. Every number here decays fast — country lists, rates and queue availability change without notice. Nothing here is sponsored, and I have no referral links to sell you.

The shape of the market

Nobody at a frontier lab is grading your ranking. The labs buy human judgment from vendors; the vendors recruit and manage the workforce; you are the workforce. Three distinct business models, and the difference matters more than the pay rate on the ad:

Outlier

Scale AI
outlier.ai

Model. Highest-volume general queue. Advertised $15–50/hr; contributor-reported reality is roughly $15–20 for general writing/review, $25–40 coding, $40–80 for law/medicine. The $200/hr figure exists for niche PhD demand and most people never see it.

Entry. Government ID, résumé, LinkedIn, an expertise verification per claimed field, an 80%-threshold reasoning test. Onboarding modules and assessments are unpaid.

Known complaints. The loudest category in the whole market: sudden deactivations attributed to "copy-paste, auto-typer, AI use" with templated support replies, sometimes on accounts that hadn't submitted a task in months. Weekly PayPal. Countries: as of mid-2026, reported closed to new Nigeria sign-ups — not banned by policy, simply not on the operating list. Worth re-checking quarterly, not worth waiting on.

Handshake AI

Handshake AI Solutions LLC
joinhandshake.com → AI fellowship

Model. Structured fellowship, not an open queue: résumé plus a short research statement, often a 20–45 min human call with a research lead, fixed term, 10–20 hrs/week.

Pay. The strongest of the three for credentialed people: ~$75–125/hr clustered near $90–100 for graduate-level work; per-task projects quoted $120–140 each; specialist listings advertised up to $300–500/hr in medicine and physical sciences. Weekly via Deel → PayPal/Wise/bank.

The gate. Leans hard on US work authorization and academic credentials, and the contractor agreement lets them withhold in-progress pay on a terms violation — contractor ratings reflect exactly that. If you are not US-authorised this is a "not for me", not a "not yet".

RWS TrainAI

RWS plc
rws.com → TrainAI community

Model. A 40-year language-services company (London-listed) with a pool of roughly 100k raters/annotators/linguists. You register as a private individual or freelancer, take language-level plus general attention/critical-thinking tests, then receive emailed project invitations. Minimum age 18. No fee, ever.

Pay. Low and plain: US/EU listings around $14–15/hr for language-identification annotation; US-based Glassdoor submissions for "AI Data Annotator" cluster at $20–22/hr (their "$36–49/hr" band is a modelled estimate, not what people reported). PayPal or bank.

Why consider it. Rare-language work pays premiums here and nowhere else at this quality bar, the corporate entity is auditable, and it is genuinely global. The trade: slow invitations and a ceiling you hit in a month.

Getting the money, from Nigeria

This is where most advice gets vague, so concretely, in rough order of usefulness:

PayoneerAccepted by the most platforms; withdraws to a local bank; the fee is visible so it's the honest baseline
USD domiciliary accountBest for larger, regular amounts; needs an opening deposit and paperwork
Grey / Kuda USDFast to open, useful for small platforms that only support ACH-style rails
WiseGreat rates, but receiving into Nigeria is restricted — fine if you have a foreign account, not a workaround
PayPalWidely offered, poorly usable for receiving locally. Never pick a queue because it "pays via PayPal"
USDT / P2PBest effective rate, real counterparty and regulatory risk, and some platforms prohibit it outright

Also: this is self-employment income. Set aside something for tax instead of discovering it in year two, and keep the ledger for exactly that reason.

Language arbitrage nobody uses

Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo, and Nigerian Pidgin annotators are scarce and the low-resource queues pay above the generalist rate for the same effort. RWS, Appen and a few specialist vendors post these on a rolling basis. If you read and write one of them well, that is a concrete advantage over every English-only applicant — far more than another "AI side hustle" signup.

Scam anatomy

Every one of these props is a thing a scammer can build in an afternoon — including a site that looks like the one you're reading, minus the disclaimers. Knowing what they fake is worth more than any rate table.

What they fake

✕
Earnings dashboards and payout screenshots. "I made ₦890k last month" posts are the funnel. A dashboard is a DOM; it proves nothing. This site can render one too — that's precisely why you should not believe any of them.
✕
Onboarding / verification pages. A cloned ID-check page exists to harvest your passport and selfie for account takeover, and to justify a "verification fee".
✕
Training courses and "assessment answers". The entire real application is public and free. Anyone charging for the gate is selling the gate.
✕
"US location accounts". Sold on Telegram/WhatsApp. ID, IP and payout country must match; someone else's identity is an instant permanent ban — and you handed a stranger your documents.

Five checks that cost nothing

  1. Does the domain match the vendor? Real invitations arrive from the corporate domain, not gmail/outlook/hotmail, not a link shortener, not a Telegram handle.
  2. Can you find the contract? Legit platforms produce a signed agreement, a payout entity name, and an invoice line item from a payment processor (Deel, Payoneer, the vendor itself).
  3. Search the exact phrase from their email. Recruitment copy gets reused across scam rings; the first hit being a Reddit warning thread is your answer.
  4. Company registry. RWS is LSE-listed; Scale AI and Labelbox are well-documented private companies. "Innovatech AI Solutions Ltd" with a 4-month-old registration is not that.
  5. Any money leaving your side, in any form — fee, deposit, "equipment", "training", "software licence", "withdrawal unlock". That is the whole test. No legitimate employer in this industry takes money from a contributor.
!
The nastiest variant targets people already working: a "QM" or "project manager" DM offering higher-rate private work off-platform, paid weekly in crypto. You do three weeks of tasks, get promised a big Friday payout, and there is no contract to enforce, because there never was a platform.

A strategy that is boring enough to work

1. Build the evidence before the money. Register on two mid-tier pools (RWS, Appen) and one expert-leaning platform. They have different queue dynamics, and the pool accounts are what you fall back on when a high-rate queue goes silent for eleven days — which it will.

2. Spend your unpaid hours where they compound. Assessment time is unrecoverable, so practise the format first (that's what this is for), and take real assessments only when you can clear them in one pass. Several platforms permit zero retakes.

3. Pick one lane and get measurably good at it. A coding queue at $40/hr beats a generalist queue at $18/hr for the same month. Two weeks on the reasoning-trace and code-review tasks here tells you which lane you already score in — that answer is worth more than another signup.

4. Treat your quality score as your savings account. Never trade score for throughput. Rushing an item to hit 40 hours in a week is how an average-looking person gets an average-looking restriction.

5. Keep the log, withdraw on a short cycle, diversify out of this. The income is real and the instability is also real. People who do well here use it as a bridge — into contract dev, into data-labeling QA roles, into in-house "AI trainer" jobs at $60–130k — not as a career identity.

6. Be honest about what the work is. It is quality-controlled reading and writing against a rubric, several hours a day, at rates that are good locally and mediocre globally. Anyone selling it as passive income is selling the funnel, not the work.
